How the rating moves

Win and you take points off the losing side; lose and you hand them over. How many depends on the gap between the two teams' averages, so beating a strong table is worth more than beating a weak one. Being outnumbered as mafia pays better, and losing as mafia costs less — the maths accounts for the shorter side.

The shape of a game

Everyone gets a secret card. The mafia know each other; nobody else knows anything. The game then alternates between night and day until one side is gone.

Night. Everyone shuts their eyes. The narrator wakes each role in turn — mafia choose a target, doctor chooses someone to protect, detective chooses someone to check. All of it happens silently, by pointing.

Day. Everyone opens their eyes and the narrator says who died, or that nobody did. Then the table talks it out and votes. Whoever gets the most votes is out and flips their card face up. Then night falls again.

The roles
MafiaKills at night

You wake with the other mafia and agree on one person to eliminate. During the day you vote and argue like everyone else — the whole job is not looking like mafia. Defending each other too hard is the usual tell.

DoctorSaves at night

Each night you point at one person. If the mafia went for them, they survive and the narrator announces nobody died. You may save yourself, though most tables limit that to once per game. Saying you're the doctor out loud usually gets you killed that night.

DetectiveChecks at night

Each night you point at one person and the narrator nods for mafia, shakes for a villager. The hard part is spending what you learn — claim too early and the mafia kill you, too late and nobody believes you.

VillagerVotes by day

No night power, and the most underrated card at the table. You have nothing to hide, so you can push hard, ask the questions nobody else can afford to ask, and pressure people into slipping.

Winning

The villagers win the moment the last mafia is voted out or killed.
Mafia win the moment they equal the number of villagers left — at two mafia and two villagers, the mafia can always force the vote, so the game is over.

The host calls it on the results screen and everyone's rating updates at once.

House rules worth agreeing on first

Can the doctor save themselves, and how often? Do eliminated players stay silent, or can they talk once they're out (silent is better)? Does a tied vote mean nobody dies, or a runoff? Is there a time limit on the day discussion? Settle these before the first night, not in the middle of an argument.

If you're narrating

Keep your voice flat — the same tone whether the doctor saved someone or not. Don't react to what you see with your eyes open. Give the same pause for every role, even ones nobody is playing, so the table can't count seconds. And use the moderator sheet on your card screen rather than trying to remember who's who.
